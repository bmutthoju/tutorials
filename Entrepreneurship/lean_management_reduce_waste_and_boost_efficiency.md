			2. What do they do?
			3. What do they need?
			4. What challenges do they face everyday?
			5. How does your product fit in their lives?
		2. The above knowledge will guide the decisions to provide the ideal product and experience for the customer
			1. How to get the knowledge?
				1. Do research
				2. Talking to them personally - helps a lot
	2. Increasing workflow
		1. Teams focus on:
			1. Increasing speed
			2. Reducing costs
			3. Improving quality
		2. Managers focus on improving the overall workflow
			1. The transitions in between all the important steps in a process could be where delays and other obstructions happen.
	3. Building quality into the process
		1. By focusing on efficiency, workers will build quality into the process
			1. By focusing on their tasks, workers will be more sure that their work is mistake free
			2. If any problems or defects are identified, they should be fixed immediately
				1. Eliminating any bugs will increase confidence in the overall process making the entire workflow much smoother
	4. Reducing friction
		1. This happens when work we are doing goes against what we should be doing
			1. We might be building features that customers don't need
			2. We might be building the right thing the wrong way
		2. Eliminating friction
			1. The right work gets done in the best possible way
	5. Enhancing learning
		1. The more people understand the work and the process, the easier it is to solve problems and deal with change

		3. **Motivated employees are essential for lean to work**
	6. Energizing workers
		1. Allow workers and teams to take responsibility for their work

		3. Reward success

		5. Help them learn from outcomes and mistakes

			2. Use the learning guide the next steps
	7. Constant improvement
		1. Energised workers keep getting better
		2. Lean teams never stop innovating
		3. **Workers should continue to look for workflow inefficiencies**
			1. Experiment
			2. Monitor results
			3. Find and try new ways of doing things
	8. Optimizing the whole
		1. Lean principles must guide the leaders as much as it guides the workers

	9. Eliminate waste
		1. Waste
			1. Delays
			2. Defects
			3. Unnecessary work
			4. Unnecessary movement
		2. Every process is unique
			1. We may be surprised how each step, each person involved, and transfer of product or information between steps carries it's own waste
		3. By reducing waste, the process becomes leaner
	10. Respecting workers
		1. Teamwork is essential for lean success
			1. Teams must find their own ways to organize and work together to optimize their tasks and workflows

		3. Leaders and colleagues must respect each other

## Section 3: Using Lean to Improve a Process ##
### Using lean to reduce delays ###
1. Waste:
	1. Time - Waiting (lean term)
		1. Someone is waiting for something
			1. Waiting for something to happen
			2. Waiting to receive something
		2. Someone who is waiting is unproductive and not doing anything
			1. Happens when businesses use production methods such as batch processing
				1. Instead of doing one at a time, they do in batches thinking they will save time and money
					1. Example: Suppose it takes 4 people to make something
						1. If first person completes a batch of items in one hour
						2. They then pass on the 6 items to the next person who takes another hour
						3. Etc.
							1. 4 hours = 6 products
						4. If any one worker runs into a problem, the entire batch gets delayed
				2. Solution: **Continuous production**
					1. Workers move a product as soon as they finish work on it (no waiting to finish the batch)
						1. Each product would get done in a fraction of a time than done as a batch
					2. Each product takes 10min so the first product is done in 40min
						1. The shorter the wait for the customer, the more valueable the experience
					3. Total time taken = 40 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 90min
						1. More in less time satisfying more customers
		3. How to reduce waiting (if there are delays)?
			1. Remove red-tape
				1. Waiting can happen if workers wait for their bosses to authorize the next step
					1. Spread the authorized tasks around to the workers
						1. **Needs proper training so that authorization happens as it should**
			2. Improve communication
				1. Provide workers with communication tools so that they can talk to each other clearly and easily to signal when the task is done and ready for the next person
					1. Mobile tools can help certain team members so that they can stay on the loop while they go 
			3. Improve technology
				1. Some workers may have older, slower equipments
					1. Get them onto the same level in terms of technology - avoiding slowdown
	2. Excess - When workers perform too many separate tasks, when combining them would be easier
		1. Professional photographers might take 100s of photos while on a shoot
			1. When they get back, they might have to apply multiple steps of processing to the photographers (different formats, resizing, renaming files, adding filter, ...)
				1. Instead of performing each step separately, photographers combine all the tasks into one using editing tools like Photoshop - They can trigger all of them by clicking a button
		2. Overprocessing & extra tasks (which is not required to do)
			1. If you write code for very old browser to access the website - if only a small percentage of users are using, it adds no value to most of your users
				1. Review your requirements and only focus on tasks needed to satisfy your users

### Using lean to eliminate unnecessary production ###
1. Overproduction
	1. Teams do more work than they need to do
		1. A worker might produce too much of an item they are working on
			1. There are more items in the workflow than the workers can handle
		2. Example:
			1. Bakers - one bakes, second one ices, third one decorates
				1. If first baker makes more than expected, but the second baker doesn't have enough icing
					1. Some cookes could get wasted
	2. Flow - steady and continuous rhythm
		1. We want rhythm in workflow
			1. Overproduction throws the flow out of balance, if one worker produces too much of something or faster than necessary (causes waiting)
	3. How to avoid overproduction?
		1. Workers should complete tasks as expected
			1. Workers might be pushing tasks too hard to get done
			2. Workers could be skipping steps they consider less important
				1. Example: Review, testing, quality assurance
					1. Cookies might not be baking as long as they need to
				2. **Proper review is required to ensure proper quality**
		2. Don't exceed expected output
			1. Some workers might become faster with experience, but they must produce the expected amount or same number of tasks in the same time
				1. With proper analysis, teams might be able to shorten time, add more tasks, or produce more
					1. The decisions must be applied to the workflow as a whole - or else, you might end up in waste
			2. Too much production by an entire team might result in too much inventory
				1. If product is sitting in wearhouse, trucks, garage, ..., the value of the product might decrease
					1. Perishable products don't stay fresh for very long
						1. Restaurants
						2. Flower shops
						3. ...
					2. More spending on the building or space to store them
					3. New products might make the old ones obsolete and outdated
			3. **Solution: Use continous production instead of batch production**
				1. We produce only the products we need and push individual products
					1. No need to store much inventory
						1. Batch processing needs companies to store more raw materials
							1. It takes more time to produce
							2. It could end up in more products than they need
			4. **Solution: Better planning and forecasting**
				1. Managers should evaluate past data and look for trends to see how product development changes over time
				2. Managers should expect spikes due to special events
					1. Example: Ramping up cookie production due to festival season
				3. Managers should expect slowdown
					1. Example: At the end of the year, because clients go on a break
				4. **The smoother the workflow process, the easier it is to speedup or slowdown production when necessary**
					1. Unbalanced tasks may produce too many items at different points in a process
						1. **Smooth out your flow and aim for balanced steady, output at step in your process**
						2. Excess resources takes up space, eats up resources, and may lose value even before we try to sell it

### Using lean to reduce movement ###
1. Waste when pieces in a workflow move around unnecessarily
	1. Raw materials
	2. Equipment
	3. People
2. Using lean to reduce movement
	1. **Transport waste**
		1. Moving waste, parts, equipment takes time
			1. It involves all the related steps and not just the minutes or hours
				1. Example: Moving a computer needs
					1. Disassembly
					2. Packing 
					3. Loading
					4. Unloading
					5. Unpacking
					6. Reassembly
					7. Reintegration (into the workflow)
				2. Risks involved
					1. You could damage the material in some way
						1. Might get broken
							1. Needs replacement OR fix
								1. Delays, and extra costs
					2. You could lose them
						1. Could end up somewhere unexpected
							1. Someone might move it back to where it was before
							2. People might think it is gone and order another one
								1. It needs proper training and sticking to a process - without it, it might cause waste
					3. You might not know which item is which
						1. If you move items around, you might not know which boxes contain new and old items
						2. With virtual work, you might not know which file is the latest one to move to a folder
			2. **Solution: Minimize movement of any parts of your process**
				1. If you can make something without sending ingredients to different places, that's ideal
					1. Example: Worker simply slides pieces to the next worker when they are done
			3. **Solution: Protect parts and eliminate unnecessary handling** - if moving pieces is necessary
				1. School lunch line
				2. Trays to transport unfinished products
				3. Virtual work
					1. Don't duplicate files or data
						1. Send them link to original file
							1. Each person can work on the same file
		2. Movement of people
			1. If you force people to move unnecessarily
				1. Introduces motion waste
					1. If it takes 30 seconds to move in one direction, you are wasting 1 min each time
			2. **Reduce motion waste**
				1. Physically place steps close to each other
				2. A U-shaped production line
					1. Ensures all workers are close together, and can communicate more easily
				3. If people move more, they get tired
					1. They need more breaks
					2. They might get injured
				4. **Consider using technology to reduce human movement**
					1. Example: Robotic arms install car parts in auto-assembly line
				5. **Consider using a remote workforce**
					1. Avoids commutes and other travel delays
					2. Businesses have embraced remote teams giving more flexibility with their schedules to get the work done

### Using lean to reduce mistakes ###
1. Defects
	1. You make mistakes and you need to backtrack a bit or start allover
		1. In a lean process, teams want to eliminate defects because of the time and resource waste they cause
	2. Defects can result in a product that is completely unusable
		1. If you forget a step, do something incorrectly, or do the wrong thing
			1. A completely unusable product needs to be thrown away - a complete loss
		2. Software and apps might have bugs, or content could be incorrect or have typos
			1. Content is easier to fix
			2. Code problem could be a bigger issue
				1. Could result in patch creation and ask users to install it
				2. **Many defects can be avoided through proper communication processes**
2. Reducing defects
	1. Workers need to understand what's being asked of them
		1. They need to know what the customer wants
			1. Needs training to workers for
				1. How to read orders
				2. Where to find everything they need to complete them
					1. Are the orders in some platform or printed out on an order form
	2. Choose some primary means of communication with workers when necessary
		1. Email, chat, project management platform
			1. Pick the most appropriate one
			2. Communication must happen effectively or the process will fail
	3. Quality control must be enforced in a workflow process
		1. There must be a review in place so that the product gets shipped correctly, and the customer sees what they expected
			1. You might have a QA person reviewing videos we make
			2. You might have a copy editor reviewing text we write on our website
			3. Inspectors pulling off items of an assembly line to ensure quality at each step
		2. Ensure workers know how it happens so that they can be confident
	4. Reduce the amount of work
		1. Focus on fewer things at one time
			1. We can be more productive, and feel more stressed about it
				1. Pressure can cause people to make mistakes and can happen in complex workflows, especially when tasks are time sensitive
					1. Example: An app needs to be updated with features, and design updates, ...
						1. Solution: **Incremental improvements**
							1. A new feature per month, say
								1. There are chances that it completes, and there will be less chances of mistakes
									1. Less defects, more customer value

## Section 4: Implementing Lean in Your Company ##
### Applying lean principles to your process ###
1. Steps:
	1. Map out your current workflow process 
		1. Make a picture of what happens from the beginning to the end
			1. What happens when the process begins?
			2. How many people work on it?
			3. What does each person do?
			4. How long do they take with their tasks?
			5. How long does the entire process take?
		2. The flow of information
			1. Where does the information go?
			2. Who sees it?
			3. Where do they see it?
			4. Do workers copy or change the info (along the way)?
				1. Example:
					1. Marketing: Propose changes to Web site - list of proposed changes ->
					2. Management: Evaluate changs - Approved list -> 
					3. Web Design Team: Check proposed changes - Are the changes doable? - Yes -> Complete changes to Web site, - no - List of undoable changes ->
					4. Marketing: Re-evaluate changes - New list of proposed changes ->
					5. Management: Evaluate changes - Approved list ->
					6. Web Design Team: Complete changes to Web site
				2. The examples shows people, tasks, information flows, and branches
		3. **Workflow Map**
			1. Include and talk to everyone that's involved
				1. You want information from workers who complete each step everyday
					1. They understand the challenges, common problems, and waste that results on any given day
			2. Pinpoint areas where waste occurs
				1. Ensure to include any problems they know
					1. When slowdowns happen?
					2. When materials run low?
					3. Is there an event that causes waste?
				2. Be as specific as possible
				3. Note down the time delays
				4. Names of people who slow-down the process
					1. Maybe they always submit the orders in the wrong format
			3. Brainstorm ideas on how to eliminate it
				1. Some fixes might be easy
					1. Using a specific subject line for emails, say
				2. Some fixes might be more complex
					1. If a worker is sick, you might have to come up with a rotation policy to ensure a job doesn't get left undone
				3. Some fixes might be difficult
					1. Workflow map can be useful to identify this
						1. It can help people to understand the whole process
			4. **Map out a new process & share it with your team**
				1. Ensure everyone understands why the changes have been proposed, and the effects you expect
					1. Each worker needs to understand how the new process affects their work
						1. Could mean doing something different, passing products or information to someone new, or completing everything in a different amount of time
			5. Start transitioning people to the new process
				1. Some workers might need to change
					1. Equipment
					2. Settings
					3. Personal schedules
				2. Some workers might need to get retrained to understand how to do something differently
				3. Communication is vital
					1. Everyone should know when the new process will start
					2. Make sure everyone is on the same page and be ready for questions when they arise
			6. Start measuring results
				1. Look for existing or new ways that occurs
				2. Make sure the new process has been implemented correctly
2. Ask yourself if you need to make new adjustments?
3. Regularly review your current process and search for new ways to improve it
4. **The more we repeat the process, the easier it will be to eliminate waste, and the more efficient the workflow will become**

### Improving your team using lean principles ###
1. Lean teams want to maximize value for the customer
2. Team determines how the team and business will be
3. A change can be a difficult thing for a team
	1. Forcing people to do different things they are not used to or training people on new things which takes time and effort
	2. New processes are unproven
		1. There will be skeptics
			1. Solution: Make communication a priority
				1. Lean teams must be honest and open with each other
				2. You want every worker to understand their place in a lean process because every person can affect it
					1. **If workers realize their importance and relevance in a process, success becomes a team responsibility**
						1. Find the best ways to **share the information and collaborate with your team**
							1. Holding a one hour meeting every week might help
						2. Team might prefer an online discussion to knock around ideas and debate solutions
						3. Team could get more value out of a mobile chat app
							1. Teammates can participate from wherever they are
								1. **Get to know your team and get them to talk to each other**
	3. Lean managers must participate and lead by example
		1. Leading discussions
		2. Listening to feedback
		3. Learning more about teammates
			1. Leaders may encounter colleagues who are more challenging because of their attitude and resistance to change
		4. Workers should continually improve their skills
			1. Provide training options
				1. Hold workshops
				2. Give workers a training budget
			2. Encourage & promote training for everyone
				1. Make training quarterly or annual goal
				2. Managers can attend the sessions to experience it themselves
				3. Workers will be excited about their future in the company
					1. They will see that you are investing in them
	4. Cross-train your workers
		1. 3 to 1 rule
			1. Every worker should be able to do 3 tasks and each task should be able to be completed by at least three of your workers
				1. If all workers can do only one job, what happens if one person is sick?
					1. Does the whole workflow stop for a day?
						1. Solution: Ensure workers have flexible set of skills
							1. We can rotate workers and give workers something different to do
	5. Optimizing the whole process
		1. Lean managers must understand the workflow as a whole, and how it impacts the entire business
		2. Lean managers should cross train themselves visiting other teams and groups to understand how different processes work
			1. Example: A development manager could learn by sitting in which a helpdesk to understand helpdesk workflow for processing tickets
		3. Ensures better use of the data
		4. **Lean managers should see what's happening in other teams and breakd own any walls**
			1. Could exist in teams helps each other
		5. Provide open and safe forum for workers to share their ideas and opinions
			1. Use their feedback to improve your lean workflow

### Building a lean culture ###
1. Lean culture
	1. A company's culture reflects what the business believes in, how it operates, and how its leaders and workers work together to accomplish its goals
		1. Building a lean culture
			1. The leadership needs to embrace the lean values & principles
				1. Needs collaboration
				2. Workers are empowered
			2. Identify values that align with lean principles & promote them (to workers)
				1. Convert lean principles into value statements for employees
					1. Eliminating waste:
						1. We welcome any idea that makes our process more efficient
						2. The broader your skills, the more valuable you are to the team
							1. You could encourage cross training and work rotation 
					2. Share and promote the values and live by them
				2. Make sure you can back them up
			3. **Assemble the right tools, knowledge, or other resources to back up your values**
				1. If you don't offer your workers the opportunities to train or shadow colleagues to learn new skills, you won't be able to reflect the value
				2. Elimination of waste requires communication tools, and culture of honesty, and openness

					2. Use the tools to promote conversations, constructive debate, and fun engagement between colleagues

					4. Let your workers know how data supports values, impacts decisions, and how it can help you optimize your workflow
			6. Apply lean to improve other parts of your business as well
				1. If you find success implementing lean to one division (software development, say), then apply lean to other parts of your business
					1. Since you have measurable success, it becomes easy to convince leaders to try it out
						1. Any new team can get of to a great start
