### What is an MVP? ###
1. MVP
	1. It is not prototype - a bad version of your product
	2. Minimum Viable Product
		1. Concept from Lean Startup framework
		2. It is the least amount of effort to sell your product, to launch and get an article in tech-crunch?
			1. No
				1. It is the minimum amount of effort and resources to get to the point of whether or not a customer will say yes (I am interested in it), or no
					1. Example: Rollar coaster park
						1. Make a diorama?
						2. Build one roller coaster?
							1. No
								1. Build a page that takes pre-orders
	3. What we do when we build an MVP?
		1. We take a broader vision for our feature or product or service, and reduce it to the absolute minimum version to get to the finish line
			1. Example:
				1. My grand vision does x, y, z
				2. Remove X
				3. Remove Y
				4. Simulate Z
					1. = MVP
			2. It could be a mock-website
				1. It may not be very functional
				2. It may not be ugly
				3. It may be broken
			3. Isolate the value the product or service is delivering
				1. Remove everything else that is unnecessary
	4. We start very small and keep adding efforts
	5. They are MVP experiments, and not the version 1 (ugly, bad quality prototypes)

### Crucial strategy for MVPs ###
1. Plenty of good ideas go through MVP process and fail
	1. Good ideas can fail
		1. Why?
			1. Entrepreneur who did it failed to strategise correctly
2. Strategies to correctly run MVP experiment
	1. Two strategies
		1. Problem #1: Your idea is too big
			1. May not be big market, but appeals to different people
			2. How to target all of them?
				1. People who cater to all customer groups end up failing
			3. **Strategy: Go Niche**
				1. Different customer groups might be interested in what we are doing, but target one (to conserve, time, energy, and resources)
					1. The MVP might be not so effective for all of the groups
					2. **Pick one customer group only**
						1. The one that is most likely going to like our service
						2. Tailor our message and MVP experiment towards them
							1. Speak directly to them
								1. We can have a lot more gravity to our marketing message
								2. We can speak to a specific group of people who really want our service
				2. Find any one group of people that go crazy for it
					1. It's better to have a deep but thin well, than a shallow but wide well - Paul Graham
						1. It is always better to find a very small group of people who really want what you are trying to build vs a big group of people who are luke-warm about bying it (you will end up wasting money and time)
							1. The small group will tolerate more (even if MVP is ugly)
								1. Once they buy from you, you can expand outwards, once you get the system running
						2. Example: Airbnb
							1. Specific group - conferences that were overbooked
								1. They need space for conference
									1. The people who really want it
		2. Problem #2: Too many features (features blow up)
			1. It becomes difficult to communicate the value of your system if it does too many things
			2. **Strategy: Go with 1 feature**
				1. Example: Diabetes
					1. Features
						1. Glucose readings
						2. Stored all things you ate today
						3. Estimations of what you should eat and when
						4. Graphs of sugar levels
						5. ...
					2. People were crazy for it because of its features
						1. Isolated one feature that people want the most
						2. Build it
							1. Example: What you should eat and when
			3. Tier 1 Problems:
				1. I got 99 problems, but 99 problems are probably not Tier 1 problems
					1. Tier 1 - Top 3
						1. Example: What are their problems. What are in top 3
							1. Is most popular feature is a tier 1 problem?
								1. People might go out of their way to validate your MVP - they are very interested in solving it
									1. No-7 or 8 is not so exciting
			4. Lots of good features is not a good idea and it is hard to communicate
				1. Solution: Communicate broader value of it

### Crafting your hypothesis ###
1. It is a science experiment
	1. Step 1: Take the idea and boil it down to 1-2 sentence hypothesis
		1. It encapsulates what we are trying to prove
2. Topics:
	1. How to make hypothesis
		1. Easy to understand, construct, test
	2. Cram an idea into core essense and put it into a sentence that we can test
		1. Build testing around the hypothesis
3. Template: I believe ______
	1. Example: I believe that old people will use online match-making website
	2. Example: I believe dog owners would pay and use a service to get the dog walked
4. Template: I believe ______ will ______
	1. First blank: specific customer (not people)
	2. Second blank: action
		1. Will sign up for your service
		2. Will pay money for this
		3. Will have a need for this
5. Template: I believe ______ will ______ because ______
	1. Because: Best guess as to why they will use it
	2. Example: I believe that _old_people_ will _use_online_match-making_website_ because _there_are_not_a_lot_of_options_for_finding_matches_for_old_people_
	3. Example: I believe that _dog_owners_ will _pay_and_use_a_service_to_get_the_dog_walked_ because _they_are_busy_
6. One or two pieces may be wrong
	1. Example:
		1. The customer group may not be the one who needs it
		2. It might need a different solution
		3. Reason may be different
7. Do it
